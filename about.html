HOW THIS ALL STARTED

It started with an old Huawei tablet.

It was my first tablet and, even though it was getting on a bit, I couldn't bring myself to throw it away. I just wanted to find a proper use for it.

At the time, life was already demanding. Work was full-on, family life was busy, and there was always something that needed doing. I wasn't looking for another hobby to take over my life. I just wanted a little project.

That small idea somehow led me back into radio.

Then radio led to digital modes, SDR, antennas, portable operating and experimenting with old bits of hardware. From there came cyberdeck ideas, Raspberry Pis, AI, Lilly OS, reverse-engineering radios, retro technology and more rabbit holes than I could ever have planned for.

Looking back, some of it probably makes sense.

I grew up in Northumberland in the 80s and 90s, and I've always had a huge love of science fiction and horror. I've read a ridiculous amount of horror over the years, and I'll happily watch old science fiction one night and something brand new the next.

There is something about old sci-fi in particular that I love — chunky computers, radios, strange control panels, blinking lights and technology that looked like somebody had actually built it in a workshop. A lot of the things I mess around with now probably aren't that far removed from trying to build the sort of kit I grew up watching on screen.

The horror side probably explains another part of it. I've always been drawn to strange stories, unusual places, folklore, UFOs, the paranormal and things that sit just outside the ordinary. Give me an old ruin, a weird local story or somewhere supposedly haunted and I'll probably want to go and have a look.

Somewhere along the way I realised that none of my interests really needed to be separate.

I like being outdoors. I like camping, gardening and growing food. I enjoy homebrewing. I like radios, electronics and figuring out how things work. I love science fiction and horror, and I like visiting strange, unusual and historic places.

So why not mix it all together?

I can head somewhere interesting with a radio in the bag, experiment with an antenna, camp for the night, visit somewhere odd on the way home, work on the allotment, brew a beer and then disappear into another technology project when I get five minutes.

I'm not a professional engineer or computer expert. I'm someone who grew up in Northumberland in the 80s and 90s and has always learned best by actually doing things.

Most of what you see here started with the same question:

“What else could I do with this?”

That's what Listening Beyond the Noise is really about.

Radio. Technology. Science fiction. Horror. Outdoors. Growing. Brewing. Strange places. Old things given another chance.

And seeing where the next rabbit hole leads.

And, after all that, the old tablet is still waiting for a job. 😂
